## 1. Adopted mission and authoring direction

**0.0.8.8 tests and refines the recursive StemThing RF Triad.** The exact constrained market, spatial and temporal recursion, stream-membership lifecycle, posture singularity, and resident execution spine landed in 0.0.8.7 are the subject. ClauseThing, scenario hydration/projection, and Studio are conforming instruments whose modernization is judged by fidelity to that paradigm and by what they let the substrate demonstrate.

This supersedes the original review's product-led framing. The portable native ClauseScript economy and both named acceptances — ECONOMY-TO-FLEET and MOBILITY-COMBAT — remain required. Their role is to demonstrate that the substrate carries a real, irregular economy with funded birth, paid movement, finite hostile damage, and durable continuation. Application features cannot trade against substrate truth.

**Every live pre-StemThing surface must converge or retire through the ordinary lifecycle.** Legacy implementation limits identify work to complete; the new StemThing paradigm supplies the design requirements. The existing constitutional surface census is the binding worklist. Historical stage-only and negative contracts remain records; every live route conforms.

**Every system landed in 0.0.8.7 must be exposed to the test environment.** CHARTER produces a reconciled exposure matrix from the Unification ADR §9 ledger and current canon §§8.3–8.4. Natural-application exposure and synthetic isolation are both lawful. Each load-bearing system additionally requires a valid instrument, a current-path baseline/characterization, and candidate eligibility in the deferred optimization branch. Closeout requires zero unexposed systems and no uncharacterized load-bearing system.

Preserve the Owner's sequencing: establish and characterize the natural application before releasing new kernel optimizations. Movement and combat remain required; strategic response selection remains optional. A measured constraint with optimization deferred can complete characterization, while the underlying debt retains its explicit disposition.

The adopted workplan shape is:

1. Admit the StemThing-first mission, full legacy-conformance worklist, complete exposure matrix, and compact construction/movement/combat contracts at CHARTER.
2. Register the six clearance class families with real graduation mechanics; protected census, anchor, and gate edits remain DA-reserved.
3. Complete ingress paradigm convergence under one admitted contract, using multiple bounded PRs where necessary.
4. Build representative workload increments that finish each introduced lifecycle and expose the relevant substrate systems.
5. Execute the finite inherited and exposure-driven measurement obligations. The opening Gu-Yang precondition remains binding.
6. Evaluate native UI through an early real-pane prototype; admit dependency changes explicitly and retain egui unless migration wins admission as a better substrate instrument.
7. Release kernel candidates only on the Owner's direction, tied to measured constraints and the unchanged authored workload.

The latest unified SimThing laws supersede older laws. Neither old closure wording, an unsupported legacy door, nor a passing historical fixture can justify weakening the current paradigm.

### F3 — Partially paid movement needs a binding to the specific local transition

**Adopted: bind the DA movement defaults into CHARTER before implementation. Proposal §3.6.**

The proposal correctly separates local work from changing whole-route potential. It does not yet say enough about the identity of the transition to which paid work belongs.

Suppose a fleet has paid two of three work units toward neighbor A. The destination, topology, or PALMA descent then changes to neighbor B. A scalar “two units of progress” is insufficient to decide whether that work remains valid. Silently applying it to B creates transferable progress that the model never authorized.

Bind partial work to the admitted subject identity, target revision, topology revision, and authorized adjacent transition, using ordinary state and boundary identities. The DA default for CHARTER freezes the local price for the currently authorized transition only; untraversed transitions reprice prospectively. Author explicit retarget, cancellation, edge-removal, blockage, source-death, and placement-refusal dispositions, with no silent transfer of paid progress. This is bounded local transition state; it does not require a route sequence or a movement-owned planner.

Also settle these details:

- A finite PALMA sweep is not a global shortest-path guarantee. Freeze the reachable envelope and stall/refusal behavior.
- Equal lawful descents need an admitted deterministic selection rule or an explicit refusal. Physical storage order must not decide.
- Consume the signed native flux in the authorized direction. `abs(flux)` would turn opposed transport into fictitious mutual progress.
- Use exactly one declared native conserved-progress bound. The current vocabulary already distinguishes RF grant, Gu-Yang available, and Gu-Yang realized authority.
- Treat transit capacity, stored movement work, CostBand remainder, partial local progress, and RF unresolved demand as different quantities.
- Record the number of distinct destination fields, their horizons, and active bindings. One newly seeded field per fleet can dominate costs even when total entity count looks modest.

The example costing 3 then 4 work units with production of 2 per generation is useful. Add a checkpoint after partial payment, then change the edge cost or target. The continuation must account for every work unit and preserve the one-local-step boundary rule.

The field laws remain those of the current Core: STEAD signal, PALMA min-plus potential, and conservative non-gradient Gu-Yang relaxation through the common admitted FieldSweep machinery. Historical descriptions that reduce Gu-Yang to saturation alone must not replace the current law. [Core](https://github.com/khorum08/SimThing/blob/435aab7e9b77515d6107172ff7eed363a9e15809/docs/simthing_core_design.md), [ActionBand composition and native authority](https://github.com/khorum08/SimThing/blob/435aab7e9b77515d6107172ff7eed363a9e15809/docs/multi-axis-ActionBand-STEAD.md).

### F4 — Combat needs one explicit generation order and two-sided accounting

**Adopted: freeze the DA V1 generation-order proposal at CHARTER. Proposal §3.7.**

The DA adopts simultaneous-Current eligibility and a single post-write terminal decision for V1. The HP formula alone had left damage caps, repair admission, repair spending, and casualty timing ambiguous; CHARTER must carry the ruled order explicitly.

For example, with Current HP 5, emitted damage 10, and funded repair 5:

- capping applied damage at Current HP before repair can leave 5 HP;
- applying net damage before a final clamp can leave 0 HP;
- testing the terminal crossing before repair can remove the target despite later positive HP.

The DA V1 proposal resolves this ambiguity in favor of a single clamped HP write followed by the terminal crossing. Funded repair can avert death. Freeze this sequence at CHARTER:

| Order | V1 authority and result |
|---|---|
| 1 | Determine eligibility from sealed Current. A later casualty does not retroactively cancel an eligible funded emission. |
| 2 | Produce finite funded output through admitted capabilities and operating inputs. |
| 3 | Allocate and deliver under finite source output and the per-target depletion bound, using the ordinary market/transfer composition. Apply declared receiver deformation once. |
| 4 | Settle applied damage and funded repair once; make one clamped HP write through admitted actuation. Preserve declared repair caps and unusable-input disposition. |
| 5 | Test the terminal crossing on the post-write HP. Emit one removal consequence if terminal; funded repair can avert removal. |

Excess or unapplied damage expires unless an authored lane owns it. Bind exact effect and repair-input semantics before implementation; a genuine generic gap receives its own admitted amendment.

Damage has both source and target constraints. Every source has finite output; each eligible target has a depletion bound. Per-target aggregation alone does not prevent a source's output being cloned across several targets. Independently distributing each source and then clipping every target can also strand capacity or change attribution. Name the ordinary market/transfer composition that owns these constraints and its exact or continuous quantity contract. No second combat settlement pass is implied.

Keep `f(U)=0` for the proposed generation-local HP service claim, if such a claim is needed. Remaining HP is already the next generation's work. Keep zero-quantity members distinct from departed streams. Apply mitigation once and retain the separate accounting units of operating energy, emitted damage, and HP depletion.

**Minimum useful witness:** multiple attackers and multiple targets; one target receives overkill while another remains eligible; a simultaneous mutual kill; funded repair at a terminal boundary; departure/reentry; physical permutation; and checkpoint continuation. Distinguish emitted, delivered, applied, and expired quantities. This is one composed lifecycle proof family, not a request for a new generic combat invariant.

## 4. Studio: whether to replace egui, with what, and at what cost

**Adopted direction: run a bounded native-UI prototype early; record the decision before implementing the large new inspector surface. Studio is a conforming StemThing test instrument. egui remains the supported acceptance client unless the prototype wins migration admission. ### 4.1 Grounded migration scope

The current lockfile has Bevy 0.16.1, bevy_egui 0.36.0, and egui 0.32.3. Native bevy_ui is already present. The central `app/ui.rs` is 3,096 lines with 229 egui references. It mixes painting with temporary-context command flags and asynchronous clause-loader integration. Window dragging, frosted bounds, settings, diagnostics, selection, and lifecycle actions all matter.

There are useful existing seams: the simulation-clock UI model, scenario-library UI model, and runtime save/load UI model have no egui references in the inspected files. Preserve and improve those seams instead of creating a parallel application model. [Studio UI](https://github.com/khorum08/SimThing/blob/435aab7e9b77515d6107172ff7eed363a9e15809/crates/simthing-mapeditor/src/app/ui.rs), [mapeditor manifest](https://github.com/khorum08/SimThing/blob/435aab7e9b77515d6107172ff7eed363a9e15809/crates/simthing-mapeditor/Cargo.toml), [lockfile](https://github.com/khorum08/SimThing/blob/435aab7e9b77515d6107172ff7eed363a9e15809/Cargo.lock).

The code already times the egui pass and can hide the main panels. Use those controls to isolate costs, while verifying what dialogs and observation work remain active. A “hidden” run is a diagnostic condition, not the accepted full UI workload.

### 4.2 Candidate choice

| Choice | Fit for the requested direction | Main tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| Keep egui, improve application/view separation | Lowest disruption; usable baseline and incremental product delivery | Does not provide CSS-authored native Bevy UI |
| Native bevy_ui with a CSS layer such as bevy_flair | Closest match to stylesheet-driven styling within the existing Bevy client | Styling does not supply the entire editor interaction layer |
| Upgrade Bevy and evaluate Feathers widgets | Potentially useful editor/inspector building blocks | Introduces a dependency migration and an experimental widget surface |

Flair supplies CSS-like styling, inheritance, selectors, and hot reload over Bevy UI; it does not dictate UI construction or implement missing Bevy widgets. Its compatibility table lists Flair 0.4 for Bevy 0.16 and Flair 0.8 for Bevy 0.19. Verify the older release's actual feature set in the prototype; do not assume every current README feature exists in 0.4. [Flair](https://github.com/eckz/bevy_flair).

Feathers is Bevy's themed editor/inspector widget collection. Its current documentation explicitly calls it experimental and unfinished. Treat widget behavior, theme ownership, and upgrade stability as prototype questions. Combining it with an independent CSS layer also requires deciding which styling system owns each property. [Feathers](https://docs.rs/bevy/latest/bevy/feathers/index.html).

A Bevy upgrade affects more than egui: SimThing's tools crate also uses Bevy, and Studio has custom render/material/text integration. Bevy's 0.16→0.17 migration alone includes render, text, UI, event, window, and wgpu changes. The current lockfile already contains separate wgpu versions; an upgrade is not automatically a renderer or simulation-backend unification. [Tools manifest](https://github.com/khorum08/SimThing/blob/435aab7e9b77515d6107172ff7eed363a9e15809/crates/simthing-tools/Cargo.toml), [official migration guide](https://bevy.org/learn/migration-guides/0-16-to-0-17/).

### 4.3 Prototype and decision evidence

Use one existing settings/inspector pane plus live observation, rather than a static button showcase. Cover a numeric input, scrolling list, selection, modal focus, keyboard navigation, clipboard/text entry, pointer capture over the map, and window/DPI behavior on the reference Windows machine. Verify source diagnostics and generation-stamped readouts.

Extract commands currently stored in egui context memory into the existing application command/state owner. Both the prototype and existing UI must reach the same session operations and observation model. Transitional coexistence needs explicit input ownership and a deletion endpoint.

Compare paused and running workloads, first display, steady visible panels, active editing, resize, and long lists. Measure full-frame tails, UI CPU cost, render/GPU cost where available, input latency, memory, and observation freshness. Hold generation-stamped commands and observation subscriptions constant for economic comparison.

Choose the native stack only if the prototype demonstrates the required behavior and a credible benefit to StemThing fidelity, exposure, diagnosis, or test-environment operation within the admitted budget. A performance-neutral result can still support that decision when the instrument benefit is explicit. If widgets or rendering integration require disproportionate work, retain egui with the conforming application/observation seam. The 18–32 day estimate is a planning judgment acknowledged by the DA, not a commitment.

## 5. How to obtain more Orchestrator-graduatable rungs

**Flatten decisions into admitted contracts; keep implementation increments bounded.** One active production rung remains the default. The user-facing deliverable of each increment can span authoring, loading, execution, and observation if its ownership and evidence are already settled.

### 5.1 Establish the routing policy at opening

The opening DA-reviewed governance change should:

1. Define an explicit graduation authority independently of coder assignment.
2. Register narrowly scoped classes for admitted ingress mapping, scenario content, Studio presentation, measurement instruments/results, and named lifecycle integrations where their laws are settled.
3. Include predictable companion surfaces: exports, owning tests, documentation, evidence, permitted lifecycle rows, approved asset paths, and manifest/lockfile implementation changes only within an explicitly DA-admitted dependency scope.
4. Preserve fences around numerical law changes, type/admission authority, sealed shader bundles, qualification exceptions, and actual gate changes.
5. Demonstrate routing with representative accepted changes and negative cases: an extra engine path, a new authority, a protected census/anchor change, a missing proof, and overlapping class matches.
6. Specify how an Orchestrator graduation finishes merge, the required evidence stamp, active pointer, and generated orientation under the existing process. Do not assume a “clear” PR alone advances the rung.

The class's meaning must be more specific than “performance” or “Studio.” A path match cannot prove semantic equivalence. Orchestrator review still evaluates conformity to the admitted law and the actual focused evidence.

### 5.2 Adopted conservative route for protected companion updates

The constitutional census and anchor updates are particularly relevant because this track now requires full legacy convergence.

**DA-adopted route:** every rung that must change protected census, anchor, or gate surfaces remains DA-reserved. Organize actual authority changes into coherent increments; allow already-admitted leaf implementation/content/UI/results work to graduate through its classes. Complete mandatory code and census/lifecycle updates in the same PR.

**The mechanically checkable census-transition discriminator is DEFERRED.** It is not part of the opening scope. Revisit it only if measured router debt materially throttles cadence mid-track, and then through its own DA-reviewed gate change with exact contracts and positive/negative demonstrations. Do not assume such a rule exists when assigning O* graduation now.

Register the six class families at CHARTER and specify merge, evidence stamp, pointer advancement, and generated orientation before any O* row dispatches. A clear PR alone does not advance a rung.
